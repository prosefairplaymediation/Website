# Compliance constraints on every post

Two Florida rules shape how these answers are written. They're not decoration —
they're why the answer library reads the way it does.

## 1. No legal advice (Rule 10.370 and the MEAC opinions)

A mediator must not offer a personal or professional opinion intended to coerce
the parties, unduly influence them, decide the dispute, or direct a resolution.
Where a party appears not to understand how an agreement may adversely affect
their legal rights, the mediator must advise them of the right to seek
independent counsel.

A mediator **may** provide information they are qualified by training or
experience to provide.

what typically happens*. Never tell a specific person what they should agree to.

| Write this | Not this |
|---|---|
| "In Florida the simplified route requires no minor children and no alimony claim." | "You should file simplified." |
| "Most parenting plans I see split holidays on an alternating-year basis." | "You should ask for alternating holidays." |
| "That's a question for a lawyer — mediators can't advise you on whether to sign." | "That offer sounds fair, take it." |

This constraint is a **gift**, not a limitation. Process explanation is exactly
what AI engines cite, and "here's how it works, and here's where you need a
lawyer instead of me" is the most trustworthy voice on any legal subreddit. The
person who says "I can't answer that one" is the person who gets believed on the
rest.

## 2. Marketing practices (Rule 10.610)

Marketing must not be false or misleading. Referencing Florida Supreme Court
certification is misleading unless it also identifies at least one area of
certification. (Separately, retired judges working as mediators may not be
pictured in robes — not applicable here, but it signals how literally the Court
reads this rule.)

**Practical rules:**
- Never write "certified mediator" without naming the area — "certified **family**
  mediator."
- Never imply court appointment or endorsement if you take private cases only.
- No outcome claims. "I've never had a case fail" is a marketing violation
- Your `{{YEARS}}` and case-volume numbers must be real. Somebody will ask.

## 3. Confidentiality (§ 44.405) — the trap

This one catches professionals constantly on public forums.

Mediation communications are confidential and privileged. **You cannot use real
case details as content**, even anonymized, even with names changed, even "a
couple I worked with once."

The safe constructions:

- **Aggregate patterns:** "The most common sticking point I see is the marital
  home."
- **Hypotheticals, labeled:** "Say a couple has one kid and a house with equity —
  here's how that conversation usually goes."
- **Publicly available procedure:** anything from the statutes, rules, or forms.

Never: "I had a case last month where…"

This is also why you should not answer with specifics when someone describes
their own live mediation in public and asks you to evaluate it. Answer the
*process* question and decline the evaluation.

## Standing disclosure line

Use in every substantive comment. Pick one, vary between them:

- *(I'm a certified mediator in Florida — flagging that so you can weigh it.)*
- *(Certified mediator in Florida, so I have an obvious bias here. Trying to give you the straight version anyway.)*
- *(Certified mediator, advanced certification in high-conflict resolution. Which means I'm not neutral about this, and also can't give you legal advice — just process.)*

The third is the strongest on legal subs because it pre-empts both objections at
once.
