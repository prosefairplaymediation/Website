# Proposed additions to the Refund & Cancellation Policy

**Status: drafts for your approval. None of this is live on the site.** Your
existing policy is published verbatim; nothing below has been added to it.

I'm not a lawyer and this isn't legal advice — these are drafting suggestions
based on the gaps your current text leaves open. Worth an attorney's eye before
they go live, particularly items 1 and 2.

Ordered by how likely each gap is to actually cost you money.

---

## 🔴 CRITICAL — 1. What happens when only ONE party cancels

**This is the biggest hole, and it's specific to mediation.** Your policy is
written as though there is one client. You have two, they often pay separately
(Split Rate), and either one can blow up the session.

Right now, none of these have an answer:

- Party A paid, Party B cancels 2 hours before. Who loses the 50%?
- Both paid Split Rate; Party A cancels late. Does A forfeit their half while B
  gets a full refund? Or is the 50% taken across both halves?
- Party A paid Full Rate for both. Party B no-shows. Does A forfeit everything
  because of B's conduct?
- Party A pays, Party B never agrees to mediate at all. Your site says "mediation
  begins only after both parties agree to proceed" — so is A refunded in full?

Proposed clause:

> **Cancellation by one party.** A mediation session requires the participation of
> both parties. A cancellation or non-appearance by either party is treated as a
> cancellation of the session as a whole, and the timing provisions above apply to
> the session, not to each party separately.
>
> Where the fee has been split, any amount retained under this policy is applied
> proportionally to each party's payment, and any credit is issued to the paying
> parties in the same proportions. Where one party has paid the full session fee,
> any retained amount and any credit belong to that party.
>
> **Where the other party declines to proceed.** Mediation begins only after both
> parties agree to proceed. If the other party declines to participate, or does not
> confirm within a reasonable period, the paying party receives a **full refund**,
> regardless of the notice periods above.

That last paragraph matters commercially as well as legally: it removes the single
biggest reason someone hesitates to pay before their spouse has agreed.

## 🔴 CRITICAL — 2. Document preparation has no terms at all

Every clause you have is about sessions and a "mediation retainer." Nothing covers
Parenting Plan Preparation ($400) or the Turn-Key Court Packet ($600, two
revisions). These are where a client most often wants money back — after you've
done the work.

Proposed clause:

> **Document preparation services.** Parenting Plan Preparation and the Turn-Key
> Court Packet are flat-fee document preparation services and are treated
> differently from mediation sessions.
>
> - **Before preparation begins:** cancel for a full refund at any point before
>   work starts.
> - **Once preparation has begun:** the fee is non-refundable, as it reflects
>   time already spent. "Begun" means the point at which your intake information
>   has been reviewed and preparation of your documents has started.
> - **Revisions.** The Turn-Key Court Packet includes up to two revisions.
>   Additional revisions, or changes requested after the packet has been delivered,
>   are quoted separately.
> - **Third-party costs.** Court filing fees are paid by you directly to the clerk
>   and are not part of this fee. Notarization is included where applicable.
> - **Rejected filings.** These services prepare documents from the information you
>   provide. Because the content of your documents is your decision, a filing
>   rejected by the clerk is not itself grounds for a refund; a correction of a
>   preparation error on my part is made at no charge.

That last bullet is the one that protects you most, and it's consistent with the
nonlawyer rules — you can't decide content, so you can't warrant the outcome.

## 🟠 IMPORTANT — 3. When is a session actually confirmed?

Unstated, and it creates the "I thought I was booked" argument.

> **Confirmation.** A session is reserved only once payment has been received in
> full. Where the fee is being split, the session is confirmed only when **both**
> parties' payments have been received. A calendar hold without payment does not
> reserve the time.

Without this, a Split Rate booking where one party pays and one doesn't sits in
limbo, and you've reserved four hours for nothing.

## 🟠 IMPORTANT — 4. Your own cancellation, and declining a case

You screen for domestic violence and coercion, and you've said you'll tell people
when mediation is the wrong forum. That has to be paid for in the policy.

> **Cancellation or declination by the mediator.** If I cancel or reschedule a
> session, you may choose a full refund or a full session credit, regardless of
> notice.
>
> If, after payment, I determine that mediation is not appropriate for your matter
> — including where screening indicates coercion, a safety concern, or a conflict
> of interest — the engagement is ended and **all fees for unperformed work are
> refunded in full**.
>
> If I must withdraw during a matter, any prepaid time not yet used is refunded.

This is a genuine trust asset, not just protection. "If I decline your case you get
your money back" is a reason to book.

## 🟠 IMPORTANT — 5. Unused time, overruns, and late arrival

Someone books a half-day, finishes in two hours, and asks for half back.

> **Reserved blocks.** Sessions are booked in reserved blocks. If a session
> concludes earlier than scheduled, the reserved time has been held for you and
> the balance is not refunded.
>
> **Overruns.** Time beyond the booked block continues at the applicable hourly
> rate, in [30]-minute increments, only where both parties and I agree to continue.
>
> **Late arrival.** Sessions begin at the scheduled time. Late arrival does not
> extend the block. A party who has not joined within [20] minutes of the start
> time, and has given no notice, may be treated as a no-show.

Pick your own numbers for the two bracketed values.

## 🟠 IMPORTANT — 6. Technology failure — you are a 100% Zoom practice

You have no in-person fallback for standard mediation. This is a live risk and
it's entirely unaddressed.

> **Remote sessions.** Sessions are conducted by Zoom. If a session cannot proceed
> or must be cut short because of a failure on my side — connection, platform, or
> equipment — it is rescheduled at no charge, or the affected time is refunded or
> credited at your election.
>
> Each party is responsible for their own device, connection and a private space.
> Where a party's own connectivity prevents them from participating, the session is
> treated under the cancellation provisions above.

## 🟡 WORTH ADDING — 7. Credits: expiry and transferability

Your policy issues two kinds of credit and only puts a clock on one of them.

> **Session credits.** A full session credit issued under this policy is valid for
> **[six months]** from the date of issue. A one-time credit following a late
> cancellation must be used for a session scheduled within thirty (30) days,
> subject to availability. Credits are non-transferable, apply only to services
> provided by Pro Se Fair Play Mediation LLC, and have no cash value once elected in
> place of a refund.

That last clause — "once you take the credit you can't switch back to a refund" —
closes a loop your current wording leaves open.

## 🟡 WORTH ADDING — 8. Florida reality: force majeure

Hurricanes, evacuation orders, extended power and internet outages. In Palm Beach
County this is a when, not an if.

> **Events beyond control.** Where a session cannot proceed because of a hurricane,
> tropical storm, evacuation order, extended utility or internet outage, or other
> event beyond the reasonable control of either party, it is rescheduled at no
> charge and no cancellation fee applies.

## 🟡 WORTH ADDING — 9. Billing disputes and § 44.405

A nice piece of internal consistency: a billing dispute should never force either
of you to disclose mediation communications.

> **Billing questions.** If you have a question or concern about a charge, please
> contact me directly at info@prosefairplaymediation.com or 561-941-0896 before
> initiating a dispute with your card issuer, so it can be resolved promptly.
>
> Any billing discussion will be handled without disclosing mediation
> communications, which remain confidential and privileged under section 44.405,
> Florida Statutes.

The first paragraph won't stop a chargeback, but showing the customer was invited
to contact you first is useful evidence when you respond to one.

## 🟡 WORTH ADDING — 10. Payment methods and refund timing

> **Payment.** Fees are payable in US dollars through the secure payment portal.
> Refunds are issued to the original payment method only. Card refunds typically
> appear within 5–10 business days; bank transfer (ACH) refunds may take longer,
> depending on your financial institution.

## 🟡 WORTH ADDING — 11. Scope, governing law, and an effective date

Your policy has **no effective date**, which makes it impossible to show which
version a given client agreed to. That's a real weakness in a dispute.

> **Scope.** These terms apply to private, pre-filing mediation and document
> preparation engagements with Pro Se Fair Play Mediation LLC. Court-ordered
> mediation is not offered.
>
> **Governing law.** These terms are governed by the laws of the State of Florida,
> and venue for any dispute lies in Palm Beach County, Florida.
>
> **Changes.** This policy may be updated from time to time. The version in effect
> at the time of your payment applies to that payment.
>
> **Effective date: [DATE]. Version [1.0].**

Add the effective date and version to the page footer, and keep old versions when
you change it. If a client disputes a charge from March, you want to be able to
show the March terms.

## How I'd sequence this

1. **Items 1, 2 and 3 today.** One-party cancellation, document preparation, and
   payment confirmation are the three that will actually cost you money, and item 2
   has to exist before you take a $600 packet payment.
2. **Item 4 next** — it's protection and marketing at once.
3. **Items 5, 6, 11** before the payment portal goes live.
4. The rest as you get to them.
